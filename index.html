About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Agronomy at Kansas State University, specializing in soil fertility and nutrient management.

My research focuses on evaluating crop responses to phosphorus and applying statistical methods to agronomic questions. My experience also includes soil water processes, soil moisture sensor calibration, and near-infrared spectroscopy for predicting soil chemical properties.

I earned my M.S. in Agronomy at Kansas State University and my Agronomic Engineer degree at the National University of Rosario in Argentina. Before joining K-State, I worked in precision agriculture, developing application maps and supporting clients with technological implementations.

Alongside research, I enjoy building opportunities for collaboration and professional development. I founded the Research Hour Collaborative Initiative within the Ruiz Diaz research group and have served in student leadership roles at K-State.

Research interests

  • Soil fertility and nutrient management
  • Statistical methods for crop response analysis
  • Soil sensing and chemical property prediction
  • Soil water processes and measurement
